# First real test run

Unit tests cover the logic (758 tests: `swift test`). These checks need real devices.

## Before you start

1. **Signing certificate:** in Xcode, open Settings > Accounts, select your Apple ID, then Manage Certificates > + > Apple Development. Without it, every rebuild asks for the permissions again.
2. **Lid open:** Apple silicon MacBooks turn off the built-in mic when the lid is closed. The screen file uses "MacBook Pro Microphone".
3. **Camera:** for 4K, set the a6600 to 4K 24p HDMI output (see the settings list below). The app has no camera Format setting: camera.mov records what the camera sends, and the Signal line under Camera shows it ("4K · 3840×2160 · 23.976 fps").
4. **Display:** keep the laptop display at 120 Hz. The Dock auto-hides (⌥⌘D).

## Steps

1. Run `scripts/build-app.sh`, then `open build/TwinTake.app`. Click the record icon in the menu bar.
2. Allow Camera, Microphone, Screen Recording and Accessibility. Quit and reopen the app after you allow Screen Recording.
3. **Screen only:** turn on "Show frame", record 30 s while you speak, then Stop. `screen.mov` should play with A1 = Mac mic and A2 = system audio (Options > Record computer sound).
4. **Both:** put a window in front and click Record on the control bar. The window snaps into the frame. Record 2 min. Press ⌃⌥⌘B once and click Flag on the control bar once, then click Stop on the bar.
5. **session.json:** `status` is `complete`. Only the camera mic has `dialogue: true`. Every track has `first_pts_host_ns`. `screen.snapped_window` is filled in.
6. **events.jsonl:** holds two `marker` events with `"kind":"flag"` and `"lookback_s":"180"`, and `window_snap`.
7. **Sync:** clap once on camera. In Final Cut Pro, line up both files and check that the clap matches.
8. **Crash test:** force quit during a recording (or `kill -9`), then reopen. The menu says the session was recovered, and both files play.
9. **Logs:** Console app, subsystem `com.lukebarousse.twintake`, category `session`.

## Start and timing

camera.mov is written on an exact 24000/1001 grid that follows real time, and both files start at one scheduled time (half a second after both recorders are ready).

1. **Frames start within one frame of each other:** in session.json, the camera_video and screen_video `first_pts_host_ns` differ by less than 41.7 ms (one 23.976 frame). In events.jsonl, `track_start` of camera_video has `start_lag_ms` under 41.7 and `warmup_ms` under 100; screen_video has `start_lag_ms` under 20.9. Both have the same `scheduled_start_host_ns`, equal to session.json `started_at_host_ns`. There is no warning "got ready … after the scheduled start".
2. **camera.mov grid:** `ffprobe -v error -select_streams v:0 -show_entries stream=time_base,r_frame_rate,avg_frame_rate camera.mov` gives `time_base=1/24000` and `24000/1001`. `ffprobe -v error -select_streams v:0 -show_entries packet=duration -of csv=p=0 camera.mov | sort | uniq -c` lists only `1001`.
3. **Measured rates:** session.json camera_video has `measured_pts_fps` about 23.9808 and `measured_arrival_fps` about 23.976, and `slots_repeated` and `slots_skipped` are 0. For a take of 30 s or more, events.jsonl has the warning that the camera stamps 23.98 but delivers 23.976.
4. **No drift, 10-minute clap test:** clap on camera at the start, record 10 min (talk now and then), clap again, Stop. In Final Cut Pro, place both files by their `first_pts_host_ns` offset (see SCHEMA.md). Both claps line up within one frame, in the camera mic and in the MacBook mic. In `track_stop` of the camera mic, read `gap_silence_ms` and `overlap_trim_ms`: report them if they are not 0 (each 10 ms step can be a click on the dialogue track).
5. **First audio sample:** cross-correlate the camera mic (camera.mov A1) with the MacBook mic (screen.mov A1). The offset matches the `first_pts_host_ns` difference within one frame, at the start and at the end of a 30-minute take.
6. **Camera clock:** in camera_video `track_start`, `corrected_arrival_lag_ms` is 0-100 and there is no warning "Camera frames arrive … after their corrected time". `clock_correction_ms` grows by about 12 ms per minute (0.7 s an hour) from the moment the camera opened, also while only the preview ran: in `track_stop` it is about 12 ms times the minutes since the camera opened (or since a `camera_graph` event with `format_reset` `true`).
7. **Warm camera:** keep the camera window on for 10 minutes, then record 2 minutes with a clap at the start and at the end. Both claps line up within one frame in Final Cut Pro (files placed by `first_pts_host_ns`). `track_start` of camera_video has `clock_correction_ms` about 120, and `late_frames` in `track_stop` is 0.
8. **4K with the camera window live:** `dropped_frames` and `writer_busy_frames` are 0.
9. **HDMI pulled during a take:** the picture repeats, the problem shows within about 2 s, and Stop saves the file. Change the a6600's output size during a take: `other_size_frames` in `track_stop` counts the frames that were not written, and the orange icon shows within about 2 s.
10. **`kill -9` during a take:** camera.mov plays up to its last fragment (at most 2 s lost).
11. **End of the dialogue track:** say a word right up to Stop. The camera mic (camera.mov A1) has the sound up to the last video frame, not a short silence after it.
12. **Startup drops:** `dropped_frames` does not count camera frames from before the file began (`frames_before_start` does).
